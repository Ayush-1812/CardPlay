MONOPOLY DEAL


 A detailed, practical rules guide covering every category in your screenshot: general,

 payment, property, action, Just Say No, Deal Breaker, rent, house and hotel, how to play,

 cards and contents, instruction booklet, and about the game.


     At a glance

      For 2-5 players. Collect three complete property sets on the table. Begin with five cards, draw two

      at the start of each turn, play up to three cards, and finish with at most seven cards in hand. The

      card text and edition-specific booklet take priority if your deck differs.



 1. How to play

  • Prepare: Set aside the four Quick Start reference cards, shuffle the playing deck, deal five face-down

    cards to each player, and place the rest face-down as the draw pile. Choose a first player; play proceeds

     clockwise.

  • Draw: At the beginning of your turn, draw two cards. If your hand was completely empty before the draw,

    draw five instead.

  • Play: Make zero to three plays. Each individual card placed in your bank, property area, or center discard

     pile is one play. Playing a Rent and Double the Rent uses two plays; playing two Pass Go cards uses two

     plays.

  • Finish: If you hold more than seven cards, discard down to seven. Keeping fewer is fine. If the draw pile

    empties, shuffle the discard pile into a fresh draw pile.

  • Win: As soon as you have three complete property sets on the table, you win. A set needs the number of

    cards printed on its property cards, including correctly assigned wildcards.

 2. General rules

  • Playing a card to the table is normally final: a tabled card does not go back into your hand. A banked

     action cannot later be reclaimed to use its printed effect.

  • Property cards cannot go into the bank. Actions, Rent, House, and Hotel cards may be banked for the

    monetary value printed in their corners; doing so uses one play.

  • During your own turn, you may reorganize your property wildcards and property sets. Keep cards in legal

     sets. After your turn, leave your display fixed except when an action gives you a new card to place.

  • The site advises leaving opponents’ cards untouched; keep tabled sets and bank values clear and visible

     to avoid disputes.

  • The site suggests two combined decks or changed victory conditions for six or more people. Those are

     optional house rules; the standard product is for two to five.


 3. Payment rules

  • When a card makes you owe money, the paying player chooses what to give. Use cards already in your

    own bank, property area, or both. Cards in your hand are protected and cannot be used to pay.

  • A banked Money or Action card transfers into the recipient’s bank. A property card transfers to the

     recipient’s property area. The recipient never puts payment cards into their hand.

  • Pay with the printed values of the cards you choose. There is no change: a $5M card used for a $3M debt

    pays $5M. If all your tabled cards are worth less than the debt, give what you have; the unpaid

    remainder is lost. If you have no eligible tabled value, pay nothing.

  • You may pay with a property from a completed set and thereby break that set. Houses and hotels may be

    chosen as payment too. The multicolor Property Wild has no money value, so it cannot settle a money

     debt.

    Payment example

     You owe $4M and have a $2M bank card, a $3M banked Action card, and a property worth $1M.

     You can give $2M + the $3M card (overpay by $1M), or the $3M card + property ($4M exactly).

     You decide, not the person collecting.



 4. Property rules

  • Build sets in your property area. Brown, dark blue, and utilities need two cards; railroad needs four; the

     other standard color groups need three. Use the number printed on your edition’s property cards if it

      differs.

  • A two-color wildcard represents one of its two printed colors at a time. A multicolor Property Wild may be

    placed alone, but it needs another property card alongside it to support a Rent charge. The multicolor

     wild has no cash value.

  • You may move wildcards between eligible sets during your turn. Extras beyond a completed set start a

    separate set; they do not enlarge the completed set beyond its stated size.

  • An incomplete property can be taken with Sly Deal or swapped with Forced Deal. A complete set is

     protected from those single-property actions, but Deal Breaker can take the whole set, including any

 5. Action rules


   Card                         Effect when played as an action


   Pass Go                   Draw two cards. You may play more than one if you have plays left.


   Sly Deal                    Take one property from another player’s incomplete set; not a complete set.


   Forced Deal               Swap one of your properties with one property from another player’s incomplete
                                       set. The values need not match.


   Debt Collector               Choose one player to pay you $5M.


     It's My Birthday               Every other player pays you $2M.


   Deal Breaker                Take one entire completed property set from a player, with its attached
                                     buildings.


    Just Say No                   Cancel an Action card directed at you; see section 6.


   Rent / Double the Rent        Charge rent from one eligible group / double a rent charge; see section 8.


   House / Hotel               Add a rent bonus to an eligible completed set; see section 9.


 When used for their effect, Action cards go to the center discard pile. When banked, they are money and cannot be activated.
  Actions that consume a normal turn play count toward the three-card limit.


 6. Just Say No rules

  • Play Just Say No from your hand in response to an Action card affecting you. Discard it to cancel that

     action against you. It can be used on another player’s turn and does not use one of your three normal

     plays.

  • A Just Say No may itself be countered by another Just Say No. Continue until nobody responds: each

     successive counter flips whether the original action succeeds against the affected player.

  • Against a Double the Rent card, the site says the counter cancels the doubling while the original rent still

     applies. For a multi-target action such as It’s My Birthday, the site describes the usual interpretation as

     canceling only the defending player’s payment, while acknowledging table variation.

    Counter example

      Alex uses Deal Breaker on Bea’s completed set. Bea plays Just Say No; the theft is blocked. Alex

      plays another Just Say No; the theft succeeds unless Bea or another applicable player responds

      again under your group’s agreed interpretation.

 7. Deal Breaker rules

  • Deal Breaker targets a complete property set, not a loose property or incomplete set. Move the entire

    chosen set to your property area, including any House or Hotel attached to it.

  • The source site treats a mistakenly played Deal Breaker against a player with no full set as spent: it goes

     to the discard pile and still counts as one of the player’s three plays. For an online game, define

     invalid-target handling explicitly before implementation.


 8. Rent rules

  • To charge rent, you must have a matching property group displayed. Play the Rent card to the discard

      pile, choose one eligible color, and charge according to the rent ladder printed on those property cards.

    One Rent card does not combine two color groups.

  • A two-color Rent card lets you choose one of its printed colors and charges all other players. A multicolor

    Wild Rent card chooses one displayed color and charges one chosen player. A multicolor Property Wild

    alone cannot generate rent.

  • Double the Rent accompanies a Rent card, consumes its own play, and doubles the applicable charge.

    Two Double the Rent cards can be played with one Rent card in a single turn (three card plays total),

    producing four times the original rent if both apply.

    Rent example

     You have two orange properties. Read the two-property orange rent on the card. A pink/orange

      Rent card charges that amount to each opponent. A Wild Rent card would charge the same

     amount to only one chosen opponent. A Double the Rent card doubles the selected charge.



 9. House and Hotel rules

  • A House may be attached only to a complete eligible property set. A Hotel may be attached only to a

    complete set that already has a House. Neither can be put on railroads or utilities. Playing each card uses

    one of your three plays.

  • The source site describes an attached House as increasing rent by $3M and a Hotel as increasing it by

    $4M, but explicitly says the combined bonus is disputed: its authors favor $7M, while they report an

     unverified Hasbro email supporting a $4M combined bonus. Agree on a rule before play and check the

     booklet supplied with your edition.

  • Deal Breaker transfers attached buildings with the full set. If a set breaks when property is used for

    payment, the site says its orphaned House or Hotel stays on the table until it can be attached to a new

    completed set or used as payment. It cannot be newly played without the required complete set.

    Cards in More Detail.

  • Use the actual booklet supplied with your edition to settle differences. This guide explains the website’s

     topic pages in original wording; it is not a facsimile of the copyrighted booklet.


 12. About Monopoly Deal

  • Monopoly Deal is a quick property-collection card game for two to five players. Players build sets, bank

     cash, charge rent, swap or steal properties, and defend against actions. The standard win condition is

     three completed property sets.

  • For a software version, keep turn plays, table zones, card ownership, set completion, payment choices,

    and action response windows explicit. Mark any optional house rules separately from the base rules.
